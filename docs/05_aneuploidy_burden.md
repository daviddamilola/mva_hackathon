# 05 — Mosaic aneuploidy burden (bulk WGS depth + allelic imbalance)

Status: **implemented and run** (2026-09-27), including the panel-gene CNV/SV part (last section). The numbers here come from
`results/aneuploidy/WGS_EX2312012.aneuploidy.summary.json` and `.arms.json`. Research hypothesis
only.

## Question

MVA is defined by mosaic aneuploidy. Can the child's blood WGS turn that categorical diagnosis into
a quantitative per-arm phenotype, and so a candidate pharmacodynamic readout?

## Methods (submission text)

**Approach.** A direct, MoChA-style implementation (`bin/aneuploidy_model.py`) for a single sample
with no panel of normals, combining corrected read depth (LRR) and allelic imbalance at
heterozygous SNPs (BAF). MoChA itself was not used, because it relies on cohort-level phasing and
normalisation that a single sample can't provide.

**Depth.** mosdepth 0.3.14 mean depth in 100 kb bins (MAPQ ≥ 20, fast mode; CRAM decoded with the
explicit reference). Bins were kept if:
- ≥ 90% uniquely mappable (Umap k100; the longest k published, conservative for 149 bp reads);
- no overlap with the ENCODE blacklist v2;
- GC in [0.25, 0.75];
- depth > 0.

That kept 26,928 of 30,894 bins. Depth was normalised to the autosomal median, and GC was
corrected by dividing by the median ratio of bins in the same 0.5% GC stratum. LRR = log2
(corrected ratio / autosomal median).

**Allelic imbalance.** Heterozygous PASS biallelic SNVs from the distributed VCF were used, if:
- in kept bins;
- outside UCSC segmental duplications (77,894 sites excluded);
- with depth within 0.5–2× the median (44×);
- not in chrX non-PAR (XY sample).

That left 2,002,664 sites. Alt-allele counts were modelled as
(1 − ε)·[½ BB(m − d, ρ) + ½ BB(m + d, ρ)] + ε·Uniform, where:
- m = 0.496 is the genome-wide reference bias;
- ρ = 0.0040 is the overdispersion (median of per-arm estimates);
- d is the per-arm imbalance;
- ε is an outlier fraction profiled over {0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05}.

For each arm (cytoBand centromeres), d was estimated by maximum likelihood with a boundary-LRT and
a 95% profile-likelihood CI. The 39 tested arms were Bonferroni-corrected (α = 0.00128).

**Spatial consistency (window-deletion test).** An arm-level call also requires the arm to stay
significant after removing the ~20% of 5 Mb windows with the strongest imbalance. Otherwise it is
reported as focal imbalance, with no cell fraction.

**Cell fraction.** For a clone fraction f, the imbalance d and LRR expected under each model are:

| Model | d | LRR |
|---|---|---|
| gain | f / (2(2 + f)) | log2(1 + f/2) |
| loss | f / (2(2 − f)) | log2(1 − f/2) |
| CN-LOH | f / 2 | 0 |

The model whose predicted LRR is closest to the observed LRR (in SE units) is reported.

**Detection limit.** The smallest f detectable at 80% power and the Bonferroni α, per arm, from
the arm's site count, depth, m and ρ.

**Validation.** A simulation test (`tests/unit/test_aneuploidy_model.py`: synthetic XY genome at
45× with overdispersion, reference bias and GC bias) passes 15/15 checks:
- planted gain 20%, loss 10%, loss 8% and CN-LOH 30% are all called, with the correct model and
  CIs containing the truth;
- no false positives on 8 normal arms;
- a paralogue-like hotspot on a normal arm is not called arm-level;
- chrX LRR −1.000.

## Development note: first run was wrong, and why

The first version of the model (global overdispersion, no segdup exclusion, no outlier term)
called **13 of 39 arms** as mosaic CN-LOH (d 0.013–0.055, LRR ≈ 0). Those calls were **artefacts**:
- In 5 Mb windows, the flagged arms were indistinguishable from clean control arms (median
  |BAF − m| ≈ 0.066 in both).
- The signal came from a few hotspot windows where up to ~50% of "het" sites were strongly skewed,
  the signature of paralogous sequence variants in segmental duplications and pericentromeric
  repeats.
- Control arms had such hotspots too; whether an arm tipped over depended on dilution, not biology.

The three fixes above (segdup exclusion, profiled outlier component, window-deletion test) each
came from this diagnosis, and the simulation test now includes the failure mode.

## Interpretation for Track 2 (hypothesis)

1. **No clonal or subclonal arm-level aneuploidy above ~5% of blood cells** (above ~2.6% for
   CN-LOH).
2. **This does not measure the MVA phenotype itself.** MVA aneuploidy is typically random: different
   cells carry different gains and losses. At, say, 25% aneuploid cells spread across the
   chromosomes, each arm is affected in about 1% of cells, below the per-arm limit. In bulk data,
   random gains and losses also cancel in mean depth and add only allele-balance *variance*, which
   can't be separated from technical overdispersion without a matched technical baseline.
3. **So bulk WGS depth/BAF is not a sensitive pharmacodynamic readout** for MVA's random
   aneuploidy. The answer to "how would you know if the treatment worked?" needs cell-resolved
   measurement: single-cell WGS / scDNA-seq, interphase FISH, or metaphase aneuploidy counts in
   lymphocytes, the measurement the diagnosis rests on.
4. **Bulk WGS remains useful as a clonal-evolution safety monitor.** Given MVA's cancer
   predisposition, a clonal arm-level change (e.g. −7/7q− in myeloid neoplasia) would show up here.
   This run establishes a baseline of none detectable above ~5%.
5. **Possible refinement:** process a public high-coverage NovaSeq genome (e.g. GIAB HG002) through
   the same pipeline as a technical baseline for genome-wide BAF variance. That would let bulk WGS
   put an upper bound on total random aneuploidy.

No karyotype or cytogenetic measurement is in the clinical document, so there is nothing to
cross-check against.

## Limitations (stated in the pipeline output as well)

- Blood-derived DNA may not reflect the tissues driving the phenotype.
- Aneuploidy levels in MVA vary by tissue and over time; this is one tissue at one time point.
- Sensitivity depends on depth (median het-site depth 44×) and informative sites per arm. A
  non-significant arm means "below the detection limit", not "absent".
- Arm-level testing is insensitive to random, non-clonal aneuploidy (see interpretation point 2).
- Single sample, no panel of normals: corrections are internal to the sample. The profile CIs
  assume independent het sites (about 1 per 1.5 kb, 149 bp reads).
- Sub-arm (interstitial) events smaller than roughly a fifth of an arm fall to the focal category.
  They are not quantified.

## Panel-gene CNV/SV (closing the modifier-panel gap)

Status: **run** (2026-09-27). Source: `results/panel_cnv_sv/WGS_EX2312012.panel.cnv_sv.summary.json`.

**Methods.**
- **Structural variants:** Delly 2.6.0 `sr`, genome-wide, run in a native linux/arm64 container
  (`containers/delly`: pinned base digest and bioconda packages). The osx-arm64 build dies with
  SIGBUS on CRAM input; BAM works. Settings: explicit reference, Delly hg38 exclude list,
  MAPQ ≥ 20.
- **Exon read depth:** mosdepth 0.3.14 over the 462 MANE Select exons of the 32 panel genes, with
  these steps:
  1. expected depth from GC-matched genome bins;
  2. exon-scale GC re-centring across panel exons;
  3. length-aware noise (σ = 0.198 log2 at 150 bp, robust MAD);
  4. z-scores.
- **CN calls:** runs of ≥ 2 consecutive exons with |z| ≥ 3.
- **SV hits on a gene:**
  - DEL/DUP: overlap with the gene ±10 kb;
  - INV/BND/INS: a breakpoint inside the gene ±10 kb.
- **SV depth check (DEL/DUP):** span LRR for events ≥ 100 kb, exon z inside smaller ones.
- **Supported SV:** high tier (PRECISE, PE ≥ 3, SR ≥ 3) and not contradicted by depth.

**Development note.** The first version used fixed exon CN thresholds (1.5/2.5) and counted any SV
span overlap. With a per-exon CN SD of about 0.34, 14% of exons fell outside those thresholds by
chance: 7/32 genes were flagged, and 105 "exon hits" came from imprecise 100–223 Mb Delly calls
between repeat-rich chromosome ends with flat depth. Both were artefacts; the statistical rules
above replaced them.

**Interpretation.** No supported structural variant or multi-exon copy-number change was found
in any of the 32 panel genes. Together with doc 04 (small variants), this extends the negative
modifier finding to SVs and CNVs within these limits:
- sensitivity is good for multi-exon heterozygous deletions and duplications (expected z ≤ −5);
- sensitivity is limited for single-exon events;
- balanced events whose breakpoints Delly misses in repeats will be missed;
- mosaic CNVs below ~30% of cells will be missed.

A candidate CN call is a lead for orthogonal confirmation (e.g. MLPA or ddPCR), not a finding.
